You are a world-class brand strategist evaluating a product name at the level of the firms that named Stripe, Vercel, Supabase, Docker, Linear, and Raycast.

Evaluate this single product name against the 5 standard brand tests:

Name to evaluate: "{{name}}"
Product description: {{appFeatures}}

═══════════════════════════════════════
TEST SUITE
═══════════════════════════════════════

A. HEAD-SCRATCH TEST (passesHeadScratchTest)
   If someone hears this name for the first time, will they be able to spell it correctly
   without asking for clarification? true = yes, false = likely misspelled.

B. SIRI TEST (passesSiriTest)
   If spoken to a voice assistant, will it be transcribed accurately?
   Words with ambiguous phonetics score false.

C. STRETCH TEST (passesStretchTest)
   If this company later expanded into 5 additional product lines, would the name still fit?
   A name tied to one specific feature fails this test.

D. LENGTH TEST (passesLengthTest)
   Is the name 10 characters or fewer (spaces don't count)?

E. CONFUSION TEST (confusionRisk + confusionNote)
   Does the name sound like any existing major brand, offensive word, or embarrassing word
   in any major language? Rate risk as "low", "medium", or "high" and explain in confusionNote.

Also produce:
- tagline: a 5–8 word tagline for this name in present tense, no exclamation marks
- headline: the TechCrunch $1B headline: "[Name] raises $100M to [do what this product does]."
- overallVerdict: "strong" (passes 4–5 tests), "acceptable" (passes 3), or "weak" (passes 0–2)
- summary: 2–3 sentences summarising strengths, weaknesses, and a recommendation
